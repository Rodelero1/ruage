# Review: D117 and D177 are each half of one rule - the ground is a point, the things are boxes

*Change: **D277**. Two contradictory decisions reviewed against the binary and the decomp, both kept in their true half,
their exclusivity withdrawn, and the option costs written down. **No code changed.***

## Performance

**Nothing executable changed.** The costs the choice is priced with, re-read rather than recalled:

- **Option A (both rows as they are)**: every 164-raw (0.008-tile) refusal and the slide that exists to creep round it -
  **82, 81, 80... raw units a tick** where a step is **800**, 567 of 1,398 walking ticks (D253).
- **Option B (the game's three layers)**: **3 red of 200 binaries** in the animals family (D255) until a leg bends inside a
  tile; then the slide goes.
- **Option D (D177 strictly, no radius-aware plan)**: acceptance repaths **24 -> 529** (D177's own measurement).

The one number the review could not settle is not a performance figure at all: a **town centre's box is `[2.0, 2.0]`**, i.e.
its whole footprint including the walkable yard, so a body tested against the box could not stand there - and it does. Either
buildings are exempt from the box path or the box is narrower than the radius table says. **Unread, and named** rather than
guessed, because D177's clause narrows to exactly that question.

## Stability

**What was attacked:**

- **"D117 is the owner's rule, so it outranks the game."** It is his rule *about a one-tile gap*, and layer 3 refutes the
  general form: the game's search answers for the unit's radii, so a 0.5-radius unit does **not** get passage in a one-tile
  gap between two objects. The row keeps its true half (the ground is tested as ground) and loses "whatever the size of the
  circle".
- **"D177 is the owner's screenshot, so it must be right everywhere."** It is right for the picture he sent, which was a
  building: `TREEA`'s box *is* its tile, so disc-against-a-blocked-tile equals the game's rule for trees and walls. It is
  wrong as a *general* unit of obstruction, because the game's unit is the object's box.
- **"Keep both, they are at different layers."** That is today's state, and it is the defect rather than the compromise: two
  measures of walkable is what produces the refusal the mover cannot fix (D254, D265).

**Found and reported rather than smoothed:** the two rows were written a week apart from two different complaints - a
prototype's stuck workers (D117) and a screenshot of an ellipse in a building (D177) - and the corpus has carried them as
compatible for a month because they were never read side by side.
