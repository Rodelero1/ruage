# Review: `last_moved` was stamped on bodies that did not move - the past test and the stander set come alive

*Change: one field, two rules unblocked. `move_bodies` now writes `Entity::last_moved` at a single choke point and only
for a body whose step was non-zero, and every construction site initialises it to the tick the body came into being. That
turns on the *past* test (D210) and the stander sets (D210b) - both of which were built, reviewed and measured, and both of
which could not fire. D213.*

## What was wrong, and how it was found

The field's own doc comment says *"the tick this body last actually moved"*, and `move_bodies` wrote it at both of its
write sites **unconditionally**: every body in the tick's delta list was stamped, including an `Idle` one and one standing
its pause out (measured 2026-11-14 on a stopped villager: its `last_moved` advanced on every tick it stood still). Two
consequences, both silent:

- **the *past* test could not fire.** `created >= moved` with `moved` always the current tick can only hold for a route
  planned that same tick, so no collider was ever "past" and the rule D210 says ends an encounter was dead code;
- **the stander sets answered "nobody".** `stander_bodies`/`stander_tiles` skip a body that moved on the last tick, so no
  body that had ever moved was an obstacle to a plan - and the only body a search treated as ground was the one a re-plan
  named explicitly.

**The first fix was wrong in one place and the tests said so.** Stamping only on a real step is right, but a body that has
**never** moved then keeps `last_moved = 0`, and `created >= 0` is true for every route - so a body spawned *after* a walker
planned looked "past" and the walker walked through a man who had not existed when he planned. Three fixtures caught it at
once (`evasion`, `step_decision`, `repath_reasons`, all reporting "the scene never met the body"); the fix is that every
construction site initialises the field to `self.tick`, which is also what makes "never moved" mean "spawned at tick 0".

## Red, then green

`clearance::a_man_who_stopped_before_the_route_was_planned_is_routed_round` was written first and watched failing:

```
MEASURED the walker came 7686 raw from a man who had stopped before the route was planned, inside the 8000 raw their
footprints need: the stopped man was not a stander to the plan, so it answered with a line through him
```

After: **6 passed** in `clearance`, including `a_man_who_is_still_walking_is_not_an_obstacle_to_the_plan`, the other half of
the rule - a body that moved on the last tick must not lengthen anybody's route (it asserts the plan is one waypoint).

## Performance

**Measured, interleaved A/B, same config and seed, on this box (2 vCPU).** The acceptance match
(`--script config/acceptance.toml --seed 7 --ticks 48000`, release, each binary built in its own checkout with its own
`CARGO_TARGET_DIR`), two runs each, baseline `main` at `4138dd2`:

| run | wall | counters | match |
|---|---|---|---|
| `main` | **42,596 ms** | `entities=133 players=1 hash=bffe63e30295ddb8 repaths=765 made_room=0 repaths_body=446 repaths_ground=319` | over at 1,857.2 s |
| branch | **42,283 ms** | `entities=144 players=1 hash=42212c8e852b62be repaths=380 made_room=0 repaths_body=121 repaths_ground=259` | over at 1,919.2 s |
| `main` again | **41,896 ms** | identical to the first | 1,857.2 s |
| branch again | **42,322 ms** | identical to the first | 1,919.2 s |

**Wall-clock: −0.7% and +0.4% - the same, inside this box's own spread.** The interesting number is the one that measures
the change: **`repaths` 765 -> 380, and `repaths_body` 446 -> 121 (-73%)**. A plan now routes round a man who is standing
instead of walking into him and re-planning afterwards, so the searches this field was supposed to prevent are simply not
needed. That is the opposite of the 2.3x D210b measured when it was built with a *broken* `last_moved`: the search failed
more often then because the stander set was fed nothing, and every failure ran the whole-route fallback.

**The world is also slightly busier, and still won:** 144 entities against 133, the enemy town centre razed at 1,919.2 s
against 1,857.2 s. So the same wall-clock carries more of a match.

**What it cannot move:** the per-tick pass when nothing plans; this field is written once per moving body per tick, in a
loop that already existed.

**Owed and unrelated:** D211's `perf.rs` at a fixed entity count (last step's note) is still owed for the *search's* own
cost - this measurement says what the match does, not what a plan costs.

## Stability

**Three fixtures restated, each with the measurement written into it - and all three because the rule now *works*:**

| test | was | now | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `collision::a_walker_never_ends_a_tick_inside_a_friend` | stander placed before the order | placed once the walker is under way | with standers live, a body seeded before the order is planned around and "the walker never came within the reach" - the fixture stopped measuring anything. The encounter is now forced, and the rule it measures (one step of overlap at the meeting, nothing deeper after it) is unchanged |
| `evasion::a_repath_route_is_pulled_like_a_planned_one` | (restated 2026-11-14) | unchanged text, but it now passes with the enemy spawned at its own tick rather than 0 | a body spawned after the route is *not* past, which is what the fixture needs |
| `step_decision::an_enemy_body_on_the_line_changes_the_decision`, `repath_reasons::a_body_on_the_line_is_counted_as_a_body` | (restated 2026-11-14) | unchanged text | same: the body lands at the tick it spawns, so the walker's older route does not treat it as past |

**What was attacked:** the two halves of the rule in opposite directions (a stopped man must be ground; a walking man must
not), the *past* test against bodies younger than the route, and the whole workspace suite including the acceptance match and
`acceptance_economy`.

**A number I had to correct:** the gate quoted when this landed as 855 passed / 25 ignored was the run taken *before*
`no_pushing::a_man_who_cannot_get_his_square_takes_the_next_tree` was un-parked; the run on the committed tree reads
**856 passed / 24 ignored**. Same tree's code, one `#[ignore]` attribute and its doc comment between them.

**Known and accepted:** `state.rs` hashes `last_moved` only when it is non-zero; after this every body has a non-zero value
after tick 0, so that guard is now always true. It is left as it is - the hash is deterministic either way, and narrowing it
would change every state hash for no rule.
