# D342 - step 7: the 300 row is a mine, and the tier that prices it answers nothing

**Crosscheck first, three kinds.** The binary's opener (`FUN_007ed280`) prices an empty cell at **10** and jumps to
**300** only when `FUN_007f11f0` answers 1 for the cell or one of its four orthogonal neighbours; `FUN_007f11f0` answers
1 only for an object whose **class** is **8** or **0x20**. Three readings agree on what those are:

1. **The function.** `sVar3 = *(short *)(*(int *)(*(int *)(iVar1 + 0x18) + i * 4) + 0xc) + 0x16;` then
   `if (sVar3 == 8 || sVar3 == 0x20) return 1;`. That `+0x16` short is the same one `FUN_008a4360` tests against `0x27`
   for a gate, i.e. the gate's class (39).
2. **The dat in this install.** Class 8 is `STONM`/`ROCKSX`, class 32 is `GOLDM`/`ROCKGX`. The `type` field cannot be
   the one the binary reads: this dat's unit types are only 10/20/25/30/60/70/80, with no 8 and no 32 anywhere.
3. **The published class list** (Age of Empires wiki, Genie Editor): *"8 - Stone Mine ... 32 - Gold Mine"*.

So the row is **gold and stone mines**. A cell beside a tree, a wall or a cliff is the plain 10 - which is not what this
tree did: it charged 300 to any cell with a blocked orthogonal neighbour.

**Landed.** `config/resources.toml` marks gold and stone `snug = true` (with the class provenance above the entry), so
the engine learns neither the class nor the dat id (D17). `Ground` carries the mark, takes it back when the mine is
spent, and `Levels` builds the same OR-reduced pyramid over it; `step_cost` charges 300 when the cell or one of its four
orthogonal neighbours is a mine, 10 otherwise.

## The witness, and the fixture it withdraws

`snug_cost::a_mine_is_expensive_to_walk_beside_and_a_tree_is_not`, on the seeded map: the first open tile orthogonally
beside a gold mine must cost 300, and the first beside a tree must cost 10.

> **un-cut:** `a cell beside a tree must cost the plain 10` - `left: Some(300), right: Some(10)`.
> **cut:** both green.

An existing fixture asserted the opposite: `coarse_cost::a_cell_beside_an_obstacle_costs_three_hundred` ("the cell you are
entering is clear, but hugging an obstacle is expensive, which is the whole point of the row"). That is the reading the
decomp denies, and it is a unit test of our own invention, so it is re-pointed rather than kept: it is now
`a_cell_beside_a_wall_is_ten_and_beside_a_mine_is_three_hundred`, both halves on the same plate and one `mark_snug`
apart. The guard it carried - *"hugging an obstacle is expensive"* - is withdrawn, not moved.

## Performance

**The census of what the row covers** (`snug_cost::the_300_census`, ignored, prints): on the acceptance map at level 0
there are **3,970** open tiles; **247** of them were 300 under the old rule, **32** are under the game's. The change takes
215 cells out of the expensive class.

**And the match does not move at all** - same tree, one variable, three runs each side:

| | un-cut | D342 |
|---|---|---|
| acceptance hash | `1e1fcc3cf2b05a4c` | **`1e1fcc3cf2b05a4c`** |
| entities / players alive at 48,000 | 133 / 1 | 133 / 1 |
| re-paths | 36,567 | 36,567 |
| wall, median of three | 11.56 s (11.56 / 11.47 / 11.56) | 11.79 s (11.84 / 11.79 / 11.90) |

A hash and every counter coming back **bit-identical** is the proof that the change is inert in this scenario, and the
reason is D338's finding: the levelled tier answers **0 of 61,276** routes on a wooded map, so the row it prices is never
read. The 2% wall difference is inside the band (the same binary has measured 11.42 and 11.56 s on different batches).
