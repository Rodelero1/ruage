# Review: the picker asks the cheap questions first, and the drained node is not a source (D280)

*Change: `nearest_like` - the engine's "which source is next" - stops asking a **path question** of every resource on the
map, and stops answering a node with nothing left in it. Two commits, one subject: what a pick may ask, and whom it may
answer. `crates/sim/tests/gather_pick.rs` gains three fixtures and `World::routes_planned()` (a counter, like
`repaths`); the engine's picker is exposed as `World::next_source_near` for the scenario language and for the pricing
fixture.*

## Performance

**The regression this was written for is closed: `the_acceptance_match_trains_more_villagers` took 330.97 s on D279 and
takes 42.62 s now** (`cargo test -p ruage-sim --test acceptance_economy the_acceptance_match_trains_more_villagers`,
debug, 48,000 ticks). `main` is 26.6 s, so the port is **1.6x** off its baseline rather than 12x. Both halves of the
change are measured separately below: they land in one commit, so each was measured on its own run and neither is
charged for the other's saving.

**What was measured first, with a phase probe and a per-call timer (both deleted; they are not in the tree).**

| tick 9,600 (before) | | tick 9,600 (after the first half) | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `decide` | 7.6 s | `decide` | 8.1 s |
| `turns` | 3.1 s | `turns` | 6.1 s (probe overhead) |
| `move_bodies` | 3.0 s | `move_bodies` | 3.0 s |

That is the quiet half of the match. The cost was all after it, and the split at 14,400 named it:

| before | | after the picker's ordering | |
|---|---|---|---|
| total | 225.9 s | total | 155.1 s |
| `plan_path` calls | 760,946 (**157 a tick**) | `retask_workers` | **59.8 s** |
| time in `plan_path` | 198.6 s of 225.9 s | `decide` | 14.2 s |
| `nearest_like` | 114 path questions a pick | `retask_workers` re-tasks | 7,369, 1,630 refused |

**Half one - the ordering.** `nearest_like` asked `node_workable` - a route - of every
resource on the map, inside the loop its own comment called the cheap filter, before the sort and the `truncate(8)`. The
expensive question now comes after: four comparisons and a distance per node, the sort, the eight survivors, then one
place and one route each. Measured on the fixture pair (`nine_times_the_wood_does_not_cost_nine_times_the_pick`): **66
routes to name one tree on a map of 66, 6 on a map of 6 - after the change, 2 and 2.** The acceptance fixture went
**330.97 s -> 155.24 s** with this half alone.

**Half two - the drained node.** `nearest_like` matched a candidate on its
column and never asked whether anything was left in it, so a worker sent at an exhausted bush was answered the *other*
exhausted bush, whose own re-task answered the first: four villagers bouncing **every tick**, 7,369 re-tasks by tick
14,400 with **1,630 refused**, at a route plan each. `retask_workers` was **59.8 s** of that 155 s run. With the amount in
the match: **155.24 s -> 42.62 s**, and the two fixtures say so directly - `the_picker_never_answers_a_source_with_nothing_left_in_it`
(answered `EntityId(9001)`, 0 raw) and `a_crew_at_a_drained_node_does_not_bounce` (**299 routes in 300 ticks -> under
40**).

**What it costs now, per pick**: two routes on a map of 66, whatever the map holds. The bound the fixture states is what
the code allows - eight candidates, one place and one route each - not a measured average.

## Stability

**What was attacked, and what bit.** Every item below is mine, found by measurement, and each one changed the work:

- **The first fixture measured nothing.** It sent one villager at a stump holding a single unit and asserted a bound on
  routes; it passed before the fix, because in that scene the node is *deleted* when it empties and the picker is never
  called - the spent-node branch matches a node that is still on the map. Rewritten as a **pair of maps** (6 trees
  against 66) with the picker asked directly, which fails for the right reason: 66 routes against 6.
- **The first bound was 80 and the scene's ring tiles were a straight line.** A honeycomb of trees four tiles apart makes
  every ring tile line-visible, so the path question was cheap and the count was ~1 per node; the assertion could not see
  the defect. The pair of maps with a nine-fold difference in wood is the shape that fails.
- **`amount = 0` is refused by the map loader, and `amount = 1` is 20,000 raw units.** Two fixtures were built on the
  wrong reading of the unit before the loader said *"group has amount 0"* and a resolved amount printed 20000.
- **The probe itself cost 0.15 ms a tick** (13 extra `Instant` pairs a turn), which is why the table above quotes the
  split and the clean timer is quoted for the headline.
- **A resource node's re-task is a route plan**, so a decision that fires every tick is a path search every tick; nothing
  in the tick was counting either. `routes_planned()` is that count, and it is what the three fixtures read.

**The behaviour this changes**, stated plainly: the eight candidates are now the nearest same-kind nodes the player has
**scouted** and nobody else has claimed - not the nearest eight that happen to have a free tile - and a node with nothing
in it is not among them at all. A wood inside a wall can occupy one of the eight and be passed over, where before it was
filtered before the truncation: same answer until a worker has eight unhidden nodes nearer than his next real one.

**The suite** (`cargo test --workspace --no-fail-fast`, run to exit): **198 test binaries, 880 passed, 22 failed, 21
ignored**. That is D279's `876 passed / 22 failed` plus this change's four new fixtures, with **the same 22 reds by name**
- nothing moved into or out of that set, which is what a cost fix should say and is checked rather than assumed.

**What else stands where this change stands** (nothing here is fixed by it, and none of it is hidden by it):

- **the crowd fork** - every collision pauses the higher id for 0.25-1.50 s and the settle no longer nudges men onto
  their places; a crowd reaching one tree seats one man inside 1,200 ticks. **Still an owner fork** (`docs/next-step.md`).
- **20 reds from D279**, by class in `docs/next-step.md`; this branch moves none of them, and `gather_pick`'s own
  `a_walled_off_wood_is_tried_once_and_then_left_alone` is one of them, red before and after.
- **three `#[ignore]`d conformance divergences** (hierarchy, radius-aware search, expansion budget), unchanged.
- **the animals arrival measure** (the two reds that were red on `main` too), unchanged.
