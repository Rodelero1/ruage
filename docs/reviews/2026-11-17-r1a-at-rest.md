# Review: R1a - the step through a body at rest, deleted; the chain needed no code, the match pays

**What changed.** `crates/sim/src/world/movement.rs`: `collide`'s rule 6 loses its **unit** half - a body at rest that is not
yet *past* is no longer stepped through; the node/building half stays (a resource tile carries no radius, so the exemption is
what lets a villager stand beside a tree). `crates/sim/tests/corridor.rs`: new, a one-tile lane with walls above and below, the
geometry where a stander cannot be routed round. `crates/sim/tests/collision.rs` and `group_overlap.rs`: two fixtures restated
(see Stability 2). **Parked on `wip/r1a-at-rest`, not landed.**

## Performance

Interleaved on a quiet box, same worktree, same pack, seed 7, 48,000 ticks, release:

| build | wall | entities | players | repaths (body / ground) | hash |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| arm in place (`83f2aae`) | **40 s** | 138 | 1 | 761 (445 / 316) | `01a0fb771ee2e75b` |
| arm deleted | **154 s** | **159** | 2 | **423** (251 / 172) | `8ca598f26684b74d` |

**3.9x the wall time**, +21 bodies alive, 45% fewer re-paths, and the **enemy survives** where the control eliminates him
(`players` is the highest surviving id). A deleted arm that was absorbing collisions means far more collisions reach the pause
and the re-plan, and each re-plan is a search; the count of re-paths falls because fewer men are re-routed, but the time rises
because the ones that do are searched against a world where nobody may be walked through.

## Stability

1. **The deadlock D242 measured does not reproduce on this HEAD, and that is the finding.** D242 deleted the arm, saw a
   2,002-tick stand and put it back. That stand was measured in **open ground**, and the pencil now bends a route round a
   standing body, so the fixture that reported it no longer forces an encounter at all. The new `corridor.rs` does: walls above
   and below, so the only ways past are the game's two, and with the arm deleted **both men arrive**. So R1a's "past chain" needs
   no code here - it was completed by the work since D242.
2. **Two fixtures were asserting the rule the owner has overruled.** `collision::a_walker_never_ends_a_tick_inside_a_friend` and
   `group_overlap::a_mate_who_is_stopped_becomes_solid` both demanded that a stopped body is *never* walked over. D241 ruling 1
   (*"follow game"*) says the opposite: a body that has stopped is past to a route planned after his last move, and the original
   walks through him. Both now assert what survives that ruling - **nobody is displaced** (the engine writes no other body's
   position) and **the mover still arrives**. This is a **weakening**, and it is named rather than hidden: the property "a man
   who is not past is never crossed" is no longer pinned by any fixture, because observing *past* from outside the world needs a
   route accessor that does not exist. That gap is the reason this is parked rather than landed.
3. **`collision.rs`'s fixture now has to be walled to meet at all** - the same trap the next-step hazards name ("the scene never
   met the body" is a stale fixture, not a broken rule). Before the walls it failed with `closest 120000` (six tiles apart,
   never moving closer): with the arm gone the walker simply goes round.
4. **`army_walk::a_crowd_walking_through_a_gap_stays_on_free_ground` is red on `main` at `83f2aae`**, before this change - it is
   R10's parked blocker (a soldier boxed in by 18 of his own mates). Named here so the suite's state is not mistaken for
   something this slice broke.

**Why parked.** Entities rise (159 against the 155 floor) and re-paths fall, which is the gate's shape - but 3.9x the wall time
and a lost win are the two numbers a player would feel, and D242's precedent is that a deletion which costs the match does not
land on a measurement somebody has not seen. The fork for the owner: take it with the price, or keep the arm and keep the
`0.09`-tile pass-through it hides.
