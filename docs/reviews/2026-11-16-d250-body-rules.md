## The four defects

1. **A builder started a house with his own body inside it.** `Task::Build` accepted *tile* adjacency, so a man still
   walking past was "adjacent" the moment his centre crossed into a neighbouring tile: he began the site from **0.02 tiles**
   outside its footprint, and `take_the_ground` then blocked those tiles with his circle **0.18 tiles inside** them - the
   crossing the rule forbids, caused by the builder. Fixed with a body test (Euclidean clearance from the footprint
   `>= radius`), which is what D183 §3 always claimed the standoff was.
2. **The site-clear gate counted unit tiles, so a body at the edge was walled in** - D183 §5's *"no unit is ever inside a
   solid footprint is a guarantee"* was never pinned. A body spawned 0.1 tiles from the footprint's edge has its tile
   outside the site, so the gate said "clear": measured **tick 25, 0.1 tiles inside the wall**. Fixed by counting bodies
   that overlap the footprint and telling the owner's own side to step aside; the first stroke now waits for the body.
3. **A farm was built from outside its plot** (rule 7). D183 §3's outside-the-footprint standoff is about a site whose first
   stroke raises a wall; a farm's site-clear gate does not exist at all (D183 §7), so the standoff was pure loss. Measured
   **-0.02 tiles**: construction progressing with the farmer 0.02 outside the plot. Fixed: the order aims at
   `World::footprint_centre`, and the work test is `inset >= radius` - the whole circle inside the plot.
4. **Imperial Age showed on a Dark Age town centre's card** (rule 8): `["villager", "loom", "feudal_age", "castle_age",
   "imperial_age"]`. `check_research` asked the age's *building* requirement before its *prerequisites*, so the answer was
   "you need two castle buildings" - true, unactionable, and not the kind of refusal `is_age_gate` recognises, so `card`
   drew the button. Fixed by asking prerequisites first: "the Castle Age is missing" is an age gate and the item is hidden,
   while the **next** age keeps its greyed button (the same test asserts both halves).
