# D85's simulation half - a recruit with nowhere to stand is garrisoned, a queue that cannot place says why

*2026-10-31. Adversarial review of the change on top of `c72ff0c` (SPEC v1.45). Performance measured, stability
attacked, and two of the findings are my own errors.*

## What the change does

`spawn_from_building` now decides in D85's order: a free tile in the 5x5 box round the building; failing that, **inside**
the building, on its own `garrison` field; failing that, leave the job where it is and record
`TrainingPause::NoRoom` on the building. The reason lives in a `World` side map (`training_pauses`), is cleared the tick
placement succeeds, and is hashed - it is state, not an event, which is what lets the panel draw it for exactly as long as
it is true (`docs/ui.md`).

## Performance

**The tick loop is untouched.** Interleaved A/B against a `c72ff0c` worktree, best-of-6 per row, same generated pack,
one process per run, alternating trees:

```
entities | this tree (ns/entity/tick) | c72ff0c | ratio
     126 |                        27.2 |    27.3 |  1.00
     376 |                        26.0 |    26.7 |  0.97
     626 |                        27.1 |    27.2 |  1.00
    1126 |                        26.5 |    26.4 |  1.00
    2126 |                        32.3 |    32.0 |  1.01
```

**What a blocked queue costs, measured by a new probe** (`a_blocked_queue_costs_this_much_per_tick`, 148 entities, a
market whose whole yard is blocked by 21 units, a recruit that cannot be placed):

```
quiet   3.83 / 3.82 / 3.94 / 3.92 us/tick
blocked 5.42 / 5.45 / 5.52 / 5.64 us/tick      -> +1.6 us/tick ~= +11 ns/entity/tick while blocked
```

The probe takes **best of five samples of 500 ticks in each half**, because this box is shared and single samples came out
bimodal (7 us and 37 us for the same code, minutes apart) - a probe that compares two halves back to back measures the
order of the runs, not the change.

**Two of those microseconds were mine, and both were found by measuring:**

1. `max_hp` and `effective_speed` were resolved at the top of `spawn_from_building`, i.e. **every tick** for a recruit that
   then turned out not to be placeable. Moving them below the placement decision took the pause from ~+2.4 us/tick to
   ~+1.7 us/tick at 148 entities.
2. `tile_of_raw` divides (twice per entity), and the occupied-tile mask ran it for **every entity in the world** on every
   blocked tick. The box is five tiles; comparing the raw coordinate against the box's raw bounds first and dividing only
   for the handful inside took it to the 1.6 us/tick above. This is the same lesson the A* pass taught: a per-tick pass
   must not pay a division per entity to reject all but two.

**Acceptance match unchanged:** `players=1` (victory), `hash=91e869fcc92d606c` at 48,000 ticks - the same hash as
`838235d`, which is the "path-equivalent" signal: on that map nothing ever needed the new branch, so nothing moved.

## Stability - what was attacked, and what broke

1. **The building's own tile must count as occupied, and the first version did not.** Only *units* were marked, so a
   market's tile read as free and a recruit stood on its roof (test 2 was green for the wrong reason: the "extra"
   villager was at `(20, 20)`, the market's own square). The fix marks every non-garrisoned entity. That also closes a
   latent bug in the old code: a **garrisoned** unit kept its old tile reserved for ever, because the old test compared
   positions of all entities including units inside a building.
2. **A building past its eject threshold must not shelter a recruit** (D37's rule is about *not* having a garrison). Test
   written first, watched failing (22 villagers against 21 - the recruit was sheltered in a wrecked town centre), then
   `garrison_room` applies the garrison order's own arithmetic: capacity, then the eject floor, then the count.
3. **A stale reason must be impossible.** The reason is set on the failed attempt and cleared on the successful one, both
   inside the only function that can advance the queue, so it cannot outlive its cause; it is removed with the building
   in the death sweep, beside `queued`, `rallies` and the rest.
4. **Determinism:** `training_pauses` is a `BTreeMap` and is hashed in id order with a one-byte discriminant. Nothing
   floats; no order-dependent iteration entered.
5. **The fixture lied twice before the tests were worth anything**, both worth recording because both looked like engine
   bugs:
   - `next_id` was read back from a world whose spawns had not been applied yet, so every blocker got the same id and
     `step` swallowed the duplicates (`let _ = self.apply(..)`). The tell was "the yard is full" failing with every tile
     still free. The fixture now counts its own ids.
   - A spawned, unattended market **decayed to nothing inside five game-minutes**, freeing its own yard and letting the
     recruit out. `[decay] enabled` is doing this - and it is very probably the "mill and barracks vanished by 17:00"
     that earlier sessions could not explain. Decay is switched off in this fixture and the question is recorded in
     `docs/next-step.md`.
6. **The gate found a pre-existing failure that the last session reported as green**:
   `train.rs::a_building_that_is_still_going_up_cannot_train` refused its house with `SiteOccupied { tile: (17, 17) }`.
   Proven pre-existing by running it in a `c72ff0c` worktree, where it fails identically. It is a stale fixture, not an
   engine fault: the footprint masks changed where the seeded villagers stand, so the fixed `+2,+2` offset landed inside
   the tree line. The fixture now searches for ground a 2x2 house can stand on.

## Still open, deliberately

- **The population ceiling (D34) pauses a queue and says nothing.** `TrainingPause` has one variant; the ceiling is a
  second reason a panel could draw, and adding it is an owner decision, not a guess.
- **`Unaffordable`** is in `docs/ui.md`'s plan and has no path that can produce it (the queue pays when the order is
  placed), so it is not implemented.
- **`insert` failing** (the entity ceiling) leaves a job stalled with no reason: a different failure with no variant.
- **A blocked building retries every tick.** Bounded by the 5x5 box and measured above; several blocked buildings would pay
  it per building.
- The retry is not gated on "something changed", because movement bumps any global counter constantly in a live match, so
  a serial guard would only help worlds where nothing moves - the cost above is the price of not guessing.

## Tests

**Four new tests in `crates/sim/tests/no_room.rs`** - no free tile is garrisoned; no room and no tile pauses with `NoRoom`;
the reason clears the tick a tile frees; a building past its eject threshold shelters nobody (two of these four were written
to catch the stability findings above). Plus **one un-ignored** acceptance test (`population.rs`, the Huns, now 20 of 20),
**one fixed fixture** (`train.rs`'s house site), and **one new ignored probe** (`perf.rs`). The workspace total is read
from the gate run: **511 passed, 0 failed, 10 ignored across 90 binaries and 4 doc-test groups** - summed from the
`test result:` lines, not counted from lines starting with `test `.
